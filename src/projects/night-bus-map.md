## The brief

Twenty-two night routes, each with its own timetable leaflet. Riders had to know
which leaflet to look for before they could find out whether a bus went their
way at all.

## Approach

I started from the question riders actually ask at 1 a.m. — *can I get home
from here?* — and worked backwards:

1. One map for the whole network, not one per route.
2. Frequency shown as line weight, so the reliable corridors are obvious.
3. Only the stops where routes meet are named; everything else is a tick.
4. A dark ground, because the map lives in bus shelters lit by street lamps.

The print version is A1 for shelters; the same artwork exports to a responsive
SVG for the web, where tapping a line highlights its whole route.

## Result

Shelter surveys after launch showed most riders could plan a two-bus journey
from the map alone, up from roughly a third with the old leaflets.
